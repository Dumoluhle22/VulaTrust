# Interview guide (issue 3)

Purpose: test the assumptions behind VulaTrust with people who run or help run a small business. You are checking whether the problem is real and who would maintain a tool, not selling the tool.

**Aim for at least 3 short interviews (15 to 20 minutes).** If you cannot reach three people, document who you tried and why it failed. An honest gap is better than invented findings.

## Who to ask

Anyone who owns a small business, manages an office, or looks after IT for a small business: a family business, a clinic, an accounting or legal practice, a shop, a school, an NGO. Friends of family count. Do not ask people to disclose confidential information.

## Before you start

Read this out, in your own words, and note that they agreed:

> I am a student doing a project on how small businesses keep track of the outside software and suppliers they use. I would like to ask a few questions about how you do it today. I won't record your name or your company's name, and you can skip any question or stop at any time. Is that okay?

Do not record audio or video unless they clearly agree. Do not write down names, company names, customer details or anything confidential.

## Questions

Ask them in order, and follow up with "can you tell me more?" Do not lead them towards the answer you want.

| # | Question | What it tests |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What kind of business is it, and roughly how many people use computers? | Who the user is |
| 2 | Which online services or apps does your business rely on every day? | How many vendors there are |
| 3 | Do you keep a list of them? Where, and who keeps it up to date? | Today's practice and the spreadsheet alternative |
| 4 | Has anyone ever connected an app to a work account without asking? How would you find out? | The shadow IT problem is real |
| 5 | Which of those services hold customers' or staff members' personal information? | Whether vendors are tied to personal data |
| 6 | Have you heard of POPIA's rule about written agreements with services that process personal information? Do you have any? | Awareness of operator agreements |
| 7 | When did you last check on a supplier's security or review whether you still need them? | Review habits |
| 8 | If a tool showed you which vendors needed attention first, what would you want it to show? | Useful outputs |
| 9 | Who in the business would keep it up to date, and how much time could they spare each month? | The maintenance problem |
| 10 | What would stop you using such a tool? What would make a spreadsheet good enough? | The differentiator |

## Using the findings

- Quote or summarise two or three points in the report. Do not quote names.
- Update the README's **Limitations** and **Intended users** sections to match what you heard. If people say a spreadsheet is enough, say so and explain what the discovery and scoring add anyway.
- With three interviews you can report what each person said. You cannot claim it represents small businesses in general, and the report should say so.
